Three different jobs, three different machines

The mistake to avoid is buying every AI computer as though it were a local-LLM GPU server. Hosted AI, browser automation and local inference stress different parts of a PC.

Shannon workstation

Cloud AI + browser automation

64 GB RAM target

Prioritise memory, a strong modern CPU, fast NVMe storage, cooling, quiet operation and serviceability. Integrated graphics are enough initially.

ForgeGrid worker

Distributed jobs

16–32 GB often enough

Builds, tests, browser clients and deterministic jobs can be distributed across cheaper machines. Hardware should be matched to assigned work.

Local AI server

Models on your own hardware

GPU/VRAM becomes critical

Only pay for a discrete GPU when an identified local-model, CUDA, rendering or GPU-compute workload actually needs it.

MS-A2 vs a Brendan-built desktop

The MS-A2 is very capable. The desktop recommendation is about sustained thermals, maintenance and lifespan—not because the mini PC lacks performance.

Copy the procurement brief

Can you build a workstation around the same budget as the Minisforum MS-A2, with roughly equivalent real-world CPU performance and 64 GB RAM, but prioritising quiet sustained operation, cooling, reliability, standard replaceable parts and future expansion rather than making it tiny? It will mainly run ChatGPT/AI agents, browser automation with up to roughly 52 Chrome tabs, office apps and development tools. A discrete GPU is not required initially unless there is a clear local-AI reason for one. Prefer 2 TB NVMe storage and an easy path to 96/128 GB RAM later.

Decision rule

Choose the desktop when its quote is reasonably close to the MS-A2 and this will be a daily business workstation for years. Choose the MS-A2 when compactness, portability or its specialised networking justify it—or when the price advantage is substantial.
